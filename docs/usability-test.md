# Family usability test (15 minutes)

A short script for watching a family member use Fair Assess NJ for the first time. The goal is to find the places where people get stuck. It is not a test of the person. Run it with 3 to 5 people, one at a time.

**You need:** a laptop or phone with the live site open on the home page, this page printed or on a second screen, and a pen. Use a new results table (end of this page) for each person.

**Rules for you, the host**
- Read each task out loud exactly as written. Do not show where to click.
- If they ask "Where do I...?", answer "Where would you look?" Help only after 2 minutes stuck, and write down that you helped.
- Ask them to think out loud ("Tell me what you're looking at and what you expect").
- Do not explain the site until the end. Their confusion is the data.
- Never use anyone's real address, assessed value, or tax bill. Use the example values below.

---

## 1. Introduction (2 minutes)

Read this out loud:

> "Thanks for helping. I built a website that helps Bergen County homeowners see whether their property tax assessment looks fair. I want to see how easy it is to use, so I'll give you 5 small tasks. There are no wrong answers. If something is confusing, that's the website's fault, not yours. Please think out loud as you go. I'll mostly stay quiet and take notes. Ready?"

Start on the home page. Note whether they are on a laptop or a phone.

## 2. Tasks (about 10 minutes, 2 minutes each)

| # | Read this out loud | A good answer (for you only) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Imagine you live in **[pick a town they know, such as Teaneck]**. Find out how that town scores." | They reach that town's page and can tell you its label (for example "Within standards") in their own words. |
| 2 | "A neighbor in **Old Tappan** has an assessed value of **$1,775,000**. Check whether that home might have grounds to appeal." | They use the Appeal Checker, enter both values, and say it "may have grounds" (borderline). Watch for: do they think it means the neighbor *will* win? |
| 3 | "Find out when the appeal deadline is, and how much it costs to file." | They find April 1, 2027 (May 1 in revaluation towns) and the filing fee, on the checker result or the Appeal Guide. |
| 4 | "Which Bergen town shows the strongest sign that cheaper homes are assessed too high?" | They open Town Rankings and name the town ranked #1, or a town with the "Regressive pattern" label. |
| 5 | "Suppose you want to know whether this website saves what you type. Find out." | They find the privacy note on the checker or the Privacy page and explain that nothing typed is saved or sent. |

## 3. What to watch for

Write these in the "What happened" column:
- **First click.** Where did they click first? Was it the right place?
- **Hesitation.** Pauses longer than about 5 seconds, scrolling up and down, or going back.
- **Words.** Terms they read out loud and stumble on (assessment ratio, PRD, PRB, COD, Chapter 123, common level range). Did they open the "i" explanations?
- **Wrong conclusions.** For example, thinking "may have grounds" means "you will win", or that a town label is about their own home.
- **Phone problems.** Text too small, buttons hard to tap, or sideways scrolling.
- **Trust.** Comments like "Is this official?" or "Where do these numbers come from?"
- **Feelings.** Signs of confusion, worry, or relief.

## 4. Questions to ask afterward (3 minutes)

1. "In one sentence, what does this website do?"
2. "What was the most confusing part?"
3. "If this were your home, would you trust the result enough to look into an appeal? Why or why not?"
4. "What one thing would you change?"

Then thank them, and now you can explain anything they got stuck on.
